# Delivery Continuity

## Overview

A destination's queue marks an item sent when the receiver acknowledges it, and the drainer does not send it again. The receiver or the sender can later move back in time: a receiver's database restored from a backup, restored to a point in time, or failed over to a replica that had not received the latest commits; a sending device restored from a backup of its database. Either move silently separates what the sender believes it delivered from what the receiver holds. This file specifies how the library detects each such move from the delivery channel itself, repairs it without an operator, and records every repair in the log.

### Terms

- **Channel.** One registration of a natively serializing destination on one sending database, identified by the sending database's identity (`EVS-DEV-event-store-open/F`), the destination identifier and the registration identifier (the event identifier of the registration event). A registration made after a deletion, or made by a database restored to a point before the registration it had, starts a new channel. Two databases with different identities never share a channel.
- **Delivery.** One batch envelope the drainer sends on a channel. It carries at least one event.
- **Delivery number.** The position of an accepted delivery in its channel, counted from 1 with no gaps. The channel numbers deliveries, not events, so a destination's filter leaves no gap in the numbering.
- **Link.** The delivery hash of the delivery a delivery follows; null for delivery 1.
- **Delivery hash.** The SHA-256 of the canonical form of the channel, the delivery number, the link, the hashes of the events the delivery carries and, for each of them, whether one of its causal parents was withheld from the channel.
- **Receiver record.** The number and hash of the last delivery the receiver accepted on a channel (number 0 and a null hash before the first). The receiver derives it from its own log and returns it with every acknowledgement and every refusal.
- **Sender channel record.** The receiver record as the sender last established it from an acknowledgement, a check-in or a resume; the next delivery is numbered from it.
- **Check-in.** A pull that asks the receiver for its record of the channel, and for every channel it holds for the sending database under the same destination identifier, before anything is sent.
- **Lost channel.** A channel the receiver holds for the sending database under a destination identifier the sender serves, under a registration the sender no longer holds, that is not closed and that no resume event the sender holds names as recovered. A database restored to a point before its current registration, or before a deletion and a registration again, has lost channels.
- **Closed channel.** A channel on which the receiver accepts no further delivery: one a skip event recovered as a lost channel, or one a sender reported as shared by two live databases.
- **Common point.** For one channel, the highest delivery number at which the sender's retained deliveries and the receiver's log hold the same delivery hash.
- **Resume.** The library's own halt, wedge and recovery that realigns a channel to the receiver record. A resume that recovered deliveries the sender lacked, on the channel or on a lost channel, appends a **skip event**; any other resume appends a **rewind event**. Either is the first delivery on the resumed channel.
- **Succession.** A fresh database that restored a reset sender's deliveries from a receiver and declares itself that sender's successor.

### Reading order

`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel` states what the library guarantees. `EVS-DEV-delivery-channel` fixes the wire shapes, the numbering, the receiver's check, its record and its endpoint. `EVS-DEV-delivery-resume` fixes the check-in, the classification of a receiver record, the recovery of a sender's own events and the resume with its rewind and skip events. `EVS-DEV-sender-succession` fixes succession. The drainer, halt and recovery mechanics these build on are `EVS-DEV-destination-drain` and `EVS-DEV-destination-drain-lock`. The verification of origin chains across a recorded fork, and the causal parents whose withheld-parent record a delivery carries, are specified in `spec/causal-history.md`; the branch conflicts a skip event fixes are specified in `spec/branch-conflicts.md`.

## EVS-PRD-delivery-channel: Delivery channel continuity

**Level**: PRD | **Status**: Active | **Implements**: -
**Refines**: EVS-PRD-destinations, EVS-PRD-ingest, EVS-PRD-library-charter

### Purpose

A delivery channel is the path from one sending database to one receiver through one registration of a destination that uses the library's native batch format. The library numbers the deliveries on each channel and links each to the one before, the receiver accepts only the delivery that follows the last one it accepted, and the receiver's record of the channel, derived from its own log, returns to the sender with every acknowledgement. When either end has moved back in time, the library detects it from the channel, repairs it without an operator, and records the repair in the log. A fresh database that takes over a reset sender's data declares the succession in the log. A destination whose receiver is not this library does not use the native batch format and is outside this requirement.

### Assertions

A. The library SHALL treat each registration of a destination that uses the library's native batch format, on each sending database, as one delivery channel, identified by the sending database's identity, the destination identifier and the registration.

B. Every delivery the library sends on a channel SHALL carry a delivery number one greater than the number of the last delivery the sender knows the receiver accepted on that channel, the delivery hash of that delivery as its link, and its own delivery hash, which covers the channel, the number, the link, the hash of every event the delivery carries and the record of which of those events has a causal parent withheld from the channel.

C. A receiver SHALL accept a delivery on a channel only when the channel is not closed and the delivery's number is one greater than, and its link equals the hash of, the last delivery it accepted on that channel, and SHALL refuse every other delivery, other than a re-presentation of that last delivery, with a refusal naming its record of the channel.

D. The receiver's record of each channel (the number and the hash of the last delivery it accepted on it) SHALL be derivable from the receiver's event log alone.

E. Every acknowledgement of a delivery and every refusal of one SHALL carry the receiver's record of the channel.

F. The sender SHALL mark a queued item delivered only on a receiver record, returned with an acknowledgement or a check-in, that names a delivery the sender made of that item.

G. After each acquisition of the drain lock, the library SHALL send nothing on a channel until it has obtained the receiver's record of that channel and the list of channels the receiver holds for the sending database under that destination, and has committed what they call for.

H. When the receiver's record shows that the receiver lacks deliveries the sender made on the channel, the library SHALL deliver again, on the channel, the events of every delivery the record does not include, and SHALL record the adjustment as an event in the log.

I. When the receiver's record shows deliveries on the channel that the sender does not hold, or the receiver holds a lost channel of the sender, the library SHALL recover from the receiver, before it sends anything further on the channel, every event those deliveries carried that the sender does not hold, keeping each event's identity, hash and predecessor link, and SHALL append none of them as a new event.

J. The library SHALL resume a channel whose sender lacked deliveries through a halt it requests itself, the drainer's wedge that honours that halt, and a recovery it runs itself, committing in that recovery's transaction exactly one skip event that records the channel, the receiver record the channel resumes after, each channel whose deliveries were recovered, the events recovered, the origin sequence positions left unrecovered, and the branch point and abandoned head of the sender's origin chain.

K. The library SHALL send the event that records a channel's resume as the first delivery on the resumed channel.

L. When both ends of a channel have moved back in time, the library SHALL resume the channel after the receiver's record, having recovered to the sender what the receiver holds and the sender lacks and delivered again to the receiver what the sender holds and the receiver lacks, and SHALL record nothing for deliveries that neither end holds.

M. The library SHALL wedge a channel, with a cause of its own and without resuming it again, when the receiver's record returned for a delivery sent after a resume names the record that resume resumed after.

N. The library SHALL wedge a channel, with a cause of its own and without resuming it, when a skip event it recovers, appended by a database with the sender's identity that the sender does not hold, abandons an event the sender authored after the latest skip event the sender itself appended for that channel.

O. A refill that follows a resume SHALL evaluate each event under the destination's filter, schedule and transform in effect when the refill runs.

P. The library SHALL provide a receiver endpoint that accepts deliveries and serves, to a caller the deployment authenticates for the channel's sender, the receiver's record of a channel, the channels it holds for that sender, and the deliveries of a range of a channel, reconstructed from the receiver's event log.

Q. The library SHALL record in the receiver's log each refusal of a delivery whose recording transaction commits, other than a refusal identical to the channel's latest recorded refusal.

R. The library's default delivery-channels view SHALL TREAT each channel a receiver holds as having, as its last delivery, the delivery its log records with the highest number on that channel, as its last refusal, the latest refusal its log records for that channel, and as closed when its log records the channel closed, deriving all three solely from the log.

S. A database that restores a predecessor sender's deliveries from a receiver SHALL record the succession in a reserved succession event naming the predecessor's identity and, for each of the predecessor's channels it restored, the delivery it holds up to; and the library SHALL refuse a succession for a predecessor whose deliveries the successor did not obtain through the receiver endpoint of a destination registered in the successor.

T. After a receiver accepts a succession, it SHALL refuse every later delivery from the predecessor sender, and the drainer of a predecessor that is still live SHALL wedge that channel with a cause of its own.

U. The library's default interpretation of authorship SHALL TREAT the events a successor authors after its succession event as continuing the authorship of the predecessor that event names.

V. The library SHALL deliver each skip event and each succession event on every other channel of the sending database, whatever that destination's filter.

W. A receiver that accepts a skip event SHALL close every lost channel that event names as recovered, and SHALL accept no further delivery on it.

X. When the library wedges a channel because another live database with the sender's identity delivers on it, it SHALL report the stop to the receiver, and the receiver SHALL record the channel closed and SHALL accept no further delivery on it from either database.

### Rationale

**What the channel is (assertion A), and why the sender is the database.** A delivery is a statement between two databases: "these are the next events I send you on this path". The sending side is the database, not the application's `Source` identifier: the database identity is minted once inside the database, recorded in its log and checked at every open (`EVS-DEV-event-store-open/F`), so a backup restore brings it back with the data it describes -- the same sender, gone back in time, even on another device -- while a reset or a reinstall is a fresh database and therefore a new sender. The `Source` identifier remains attribution in provenance. The destination registration is part of the channel because a destination deleted and registered again starts a new registration with its own schedule, filter and queue (`EVS-PRD-destinations/O`); its first delivery is number 1 again. A destination that serializes through an application transform speaks a format this library does not receive, so it has no receiver that could hold a record, and its loss detection is the application's reconciliation with that system; the consumer guide states this boundary.

**Why number deliveries, not events (assertions B and C)?** A destination's filter leaves out events by design, so the events a receiver gets from a sender have gaps that say nothing about loss, and the sender's origin chain cannot serve as a gap detector. The channel numbers what it sends: every accepted delivery is the one after the last, whatever the filter left out. The link binds each delivery to its predecessor's content, so a receiver cannot be walked onto a different history with the right number. The delivery hash also covers which events have a causal parent the channel withheld, because a receiver marks aggregates by that record and refuses a delivery its log contradicts. This is a Layer 1 claim about the receiver's log, and it holds under the storage precondition (`EVS-PRD-destinations/L`): every delivery the receiver accepted on a channel follows, by number and link, the one it accepted before, with no exception path; there is no gap event. The receiver's check reads a working copy of its record, and the library's check of the receiver's records walks each channel's accepted deliveries in the log and reports any break of number or link (`EVS-DEV-delivery-channel/H`), so the claim is verifiable from the log rather than taken from the working copy. What the receiver cannot establish is that the sender's record is honest about the events it chose to send, which is what the filter decides and the sender is trusted for.

**Why is the receiver's record derived from its log (assertion D)?** The record decides what the receiver accepts next. If it lived only in a side table, a restore of the receiver's database could bring back a record that does not match the events restored with it, and a failover could promote a replica whose record ran ahead of its log. Deriving it from the log makes the record consistent with the events it describes under every restore: the record goes back exactly as far as the events do. The receiver keeps a working copy for speed, written in the same transaction as the events, and the library offers a check of that copy against the log.

**Why the record on every acknowledgement and refusal (assertions E and F)?** A sender learns that the receiver moved back in time only when the receiver tells it. Returning the record with every acknowledgement costs nothing extra and needs no probe on a timer: a sender learns at its next ordinary delivery. Checking that the acknowledgement names the delivery sent makes the acknowledgement evidence rather than a status code: a transport that drops the acknowledgement's body cannot mark an item delivered, and an acknowledgement for some other delivery is treated as the receiver's statement of where the channel stands.

**Why check in after each acquisition of the drain lock (assertion G)?** A sender restored from a backup cannot know it at boot: the restored database is consistent with itself. And a sender with nothing to send would not talk to its receiver for as long as it stays idle, so a receiver restored meanwhile would keep its loss, and a restored sender would not get its lost entries back, until the next local event. Every restore of a sending database ends the drain lock's tenure: a device restore starts a new process, and a Postgres point-in-time restore or failover ends every session, the lock session included; the next drainer's first act is to acquire the lock. Checking in at each acquisition therefore covers every process start, and every takeover by a standby process, at the cost of one small request per channel per acquisition. Sending the head and reading the refusal would detect a regression only when there is a head to send, and would put a post-restore delivery on the wire before the sender knows where the channel stands. The check-in also lists the receiver's other channels of the sender under the same destination: a database restored to a point before its current registration registers the destination again under a new registration, and only the receiver can tell it that a channel of the earlier registration holds its lost events. When a check-in's decision is held back by an operator's halt or a standing wedge, the drainer checks in again once that is cleared, so a long-running process is not left holding a channel until its next acquisition.

**Why deliver again when the receiver is behind (assertion H)?** The sender keeps every delivered queue item as the delivery record (`EVS-PRD-destinations/O`), so it knows which of its deliveries the receiver's record no longer includes. The resume rewinds the fill below their events, the refill enqueues them again, and the receiver deduplicates the ones it still holds by event identity. The log records the resume in a rewind event, which is also the first delivery on the resumed channel, so both ends record that the receiver was behind.

**Why recover a sender's own events from the receiver (assertion I)?** A restored sender's lost events live on at the receiver, which holds what it received on the channel. The sender pulls them back and stores them as the events they are: same identity, same hash, same predecessor link, carrying the receiver's hop and its own. Appending them again as new events would give them new identities and hashes and forge a second authorship of the same entries. Ordinary ingest keeps refusing a database's own events coming back (`EVS-PRD-ingest/H`); the recovery is a separate, explicit path, open only to the drainer during a resume and only for deliveries on the channel being resumed or on a lost channel of the same destination.

**Why a skip event, committed in the recovery (assertion J)?** The restored sender's origin chain has forked: events it appended after the restore and before it learned of the restore reuse origin sequence positions and link to the last event both branches share. The skip event makes the fork explicit and permanent in the log: where the chain branched, what the abandoned branch's head was, which channels the recovery read, which of the abandoned branch's events were recovered, and which positions of it no end holds (events the channel's filter never sent and the restore lost). A verifier accepts a fork of the origin chain only where a skip event lists its successors. The resume reuses the library's halt, wedge, recovery and refill (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/F`) rather than a second queue-rewriting path: the halt stops the drainer at a head without racing a send, the wedge records the stop, and the recovery retires the post-restore items and refills them numbered after the receiver's record, so rows queued before the restore was detected are never renumbered in place.

**Why is the resume event the first delivery (assertion K)?** A receiver needs to know that a sender moved back in time, and from which of its own records the channel continues, before it ingests anything the sender built afterwards; otherwise a receiver-side verifier would meet the fork before its explanation. Sending the resume event first makes the receiver's log record the explanation at the point the channel resumes.

**Why no gap event when both ends moved back (assertion L)?** The channel continues from the receiver's record, because the receiver refuses anything else. Each end first restores to the other what it lacks: the sender recovers the receiver's deliveries after the common point, and the refill delivers again the sender's deliveries after it. Deliveries that neither end holds are unknown to both: nothing in either log refers to them, so neither chain has a break to explain, and a gap event would record a claim about deliveries no one can name.

**Why wedge on a repeated record (assertion M)?** A resume realigns the channel to the receiver's record. If the receiver answers the first delivery after the resume with the same record again, the resume did not help, and resuming again would loop. The sender remembers the record each resume resumed after, and treats that answer as an inconsistency to stop at, with a cause an operator reads from the wedge event.

**Why wedge when a skip of the sender's identity abandons what the sender authored after its own skip (assertion N)?** One live source per sender identity is a precondition: the library ties a channel to one database, and different senders' events are disjoint. A database cloned while live (a copied device image run beside the original) breaks it: both clones deliver on one channel, and each sees the other's deliveries as a regression of its own. The first resume by either clone is indistinguishable from a restore -- a restored database is a snapshot of a clone whose original is gone -- so it is recorded as a skip. The two cannot be told apart until the pattern repeats: the other clone resumes in turn over what the first appended after its skip, and when the first recovers that second skip it finds its own post-skip events abandoned by a database with its identity. A restored database recovering a skip appended by its own later history does not meet this, because it holds no skip of its own after the events that skip abandons. The rule stops at the second alternation, never loops, and errs towards stopping: a database restored more than once, to a backup taken after one of its own resumes and before a later one, meets the same pattern and waits for an operator. The skip events the clones appended before the stop remain in the log, and the channel's closure (assertion X) is the later record that supersedes them.

**Why does the refill apply the current configuration (assertion O)?** A resume is the library's recovery, and a recovery is "discard and rebuild from the log" (`EVS-PRD-destinations/N`): the events it re-enqueues are evaluated as any new event would be, under the filter, schedule and transform the draining process declares at the refill. A change of configuration between the original deliveries and the resume therefore shows in what is delivered again.

**Why the library's own receiver endpoint (assertion P)?** The recovery can use what the receiver serves only as far as it can check it, and it can check it only when the receiver serves it the way the library defines: every delivery reconstructed from the receiver's log, with its number, link and hash and every event as the receiver stored it, including the events that record deletions. A range served from current view rows could not bring back an entry that was later deleted, and a hand-built download envelope carries no channel. The endpoint is transport-neutral: the deployment's own authentication maps a caller to the sender identities it may deliver for and read, and the endpoint refuses any other channel.

**Why record refusals, and a default view (assertions Q and R)?** A refusal is the receiver's own operation, so it is recorded in the receiver's log like its other audits; operators need to see which senders are behind, regressed or closed. A sender that repeats the same refused delivery -- a non-conforming client, a transport that ignores the refusal, a clone before detection -- would otherwise append one audit per attempt without bound; recording only a refusal that differs from the channel's latest keeps the log bounded by what changed, and the view, which shows only the latest refusal, loses nothing. The default view is a Layer 2 convention: the library's reading of each channel's last delivery, last refusal and closure from the log; an application that wants another reading folds the same log itself.

**Why succession, and why only from an authenticated receiver (assertions S to U)?** A reset device, or a reinstalled one, is a fresh database with a new identity; if it restores its predecessor's data from a receiver, the restored events keep their predecessor identity and hash (they are ingested, not re-authored), and the successor's later edits of those entries would otherwise read as a second editor on the predecessor's aggregates. The succession event states the continuity in the log. It is a reserved event only the library appends, and only in the transaction that completes a restore through a registered destination's receiver endpoint, which the deployment authenticates: a succession claimed for data obtained elsewhere is refused, because the library then has no record that the claimed predecessor's deliveries are the ones the receiver holds. Accepting the succession closes the predecessor's channels at the receiver: the predecessor was reset, so a delivery from it afterwards comes from a second live source, and its channel wedges with its own cause. The single-source-per-aggregate invariant then reads: an aggregate has one live source at a time, the predecessor up to the deliveries its successor restored and the successor from its succession event on. Continuity of authorship is a Layer 2 convention the library's default interpretations apply; the Layer 1 facts are the succession event and the deliveries it names.

**Why deliver skip and succession events on every channel (assertion V)?** A restore affects the sender's whole origin chain, not one channel: every receiver that holds events of either branch needs the record of where the chain forked, or its verifier meets an unexplained fork, and a receiver serving the default views needs the branch conflicts the skip event fixes. A sender with several channels recovers on each as each answers, so a later skip can record conflicts an earlier one could not see; delivering every skip on every channel lets each receiver hold all of them, and the conflict set a receiver derives is the same on every receiver once each holds every skip.

**Why close a lost channel (assertion W)?** Once the sender has recovered a lost channel and recorded it in a skip event, the channel belongs to a registration the sender no longer has; nothing legitimate delivers on it again. Closing it makes a delivery on it afterwards -- from a clone still holding that registration -- a refusal the other database can act on, rather than an acceptance that silently extends a channel no one resumes.

**Why close a shared channel for both databases (assertion X)?** The clone that detects the sharing wedges, but the other clone has not detected anything and would keep delivering onto a channel whose history now interleaves two databases. The detecting drainer reports the stop to the receiver, which records the closure and refuses both clones from then on; the other clone's next delivery or check-in is refused as on a closed channel and wedges with the same cause. An operator resolves it by retiring one database; the other registers the destination again, which starts a new channel.

**Trust.** The receiver's record is a claim by an authenticated endpoint, carried by the destination's transport, and so is every delivery a pull serves. The checks the sender runs on a served delivery -- its chain by link from a delivery the sender retains (or from delivery 1's null link), its recomputed hash, and each event's integrity and reconstruction to the hash the delivery lists -- prove that what the receiver served is internally consistent and continues the sender's own delivery record; they do not prove that the sender authored the events. The hashes are unkeyed and nothing in an event is signed, so a receiver that holds the common point's hash can build deliveries that pass every check and carry events it made up under the sender's identity. The receiver is therefore trusted not to fabricate events carrying the sender's identity, or a predecessor's identity in a succession: the recovery and the restore store what it serves as that identity's history, and the branch conflicts a skip event records are computed from it. It is also trusted to serve every delivery it accepted, and not to claim a record ahead of its log. Every adjustment a record causes is an event in the sender's log, so a wrong claim is visible and attributable; a receiver that claims too little receives the deliveries again, and one that claims more than it can serve wedges the channel. The destination transport, extended to the receiver's records and served deliveries, and the deployment's authentication binding a caller to sender identities, are the trusted inputs this widens.

## EVS-DEV-delivery-channel: Delivery channel mechanics

**Level**: DEV | **Status**: Active | **Implements**: -
**Refines**: EVS-PRD-delivery-channel

### Purpose

This requirement holds the mechanics of a delivery channel on both ends: which destinations are channels, how the delivery hash is computed, the sender's channel record and when the drainer assigns a delivery's number and link, the batch envelope's fields, the receiver's check of a delivery, the accepted-delivery audit and per-event stamp the receiver writes and the record it derives from them, the acknowledgement and refusal bodies and how the drainer maps them, the receiver's refusal and closure audits, the default delivery-channels view, and the receiver endpoint with its pull, its channel listing and its shared-channel report.

### Assertions

A. The library SHALL apply the channel mechanics of this requirement to every destination that serializes natively and to no other destination, and SHALL refuse, before anything is written, to register a destination that serializes natively and does not implement the channel pull and report operations.

B. The library SHALL compute a delivery's hash as the SHA-256, in lowercase hexadecimal, of the canonical JSON of an object with exactly the keys `channel` (an object with exactly `sender_database_id`, `destination_id` and `registration_id`), `delivery_number`, `previous_delivery_hash` (null for delivery 1), `event_hashes` (the `event_hash` of each event the delivery carries, in the order it carries them) and `parent_withheld` (for each event the delivery carries, in the same order, the boolean the delivery records for whether one of its parents was withheld from the channel).

C. The library SHALL keep, for each channel, a sender channel record holding the number and hash of the receiver record the sender last established, the receiver database identity the receiver's latest response named, the drain epoch of the channel's last completed check-in, and the receiver record the channel's latest resume resumed after; SHALL write it with number 0, a null hash, no receiver identity, no check-in epoch and no resume record when a registration is written; SHALL change it only in the transaction that commits an acknowledgement outcome, a check-in or a resume recovery on that channel; and a deletion of the destination SHALL remove it.

D. The drainer SHALL assign a delivery's number (the sender channel record's number plus one) and link (the record's hash) in the pre-send fence transaction, from the sender channel record read there, SHALL start the send only when that record equals the one the payload was built from, SHALL write the delivery number and delivery hash in the send fence record that transaction writes and on the attempt the send produces, and the change that marks a queue item sent SHALL record the delivery number and delivery hash it was acknowledged under.

E. A native batch envelope SHALL carry exactly the keys `batch_format_version` (`"3"`), `batch_id`, `sender_hop`, `sender_identifier`, `sender_software_version`, `sent_at`, `channel` (as in assertion B), `delivery_number`, `previous_delivery_hash`, `delivery_hash`, `events` (at least one) and `parent_withheld` (one boolean per event, in the order of `events`); and ingest SHALL refuse, by name and before any write, a batch in any other format, one whose `parent_withheld` does not hold exactly one boolean per event, one whose `delivery_hash` is not the hash of its channel, number, link, events and withheld-parent record, and one that carries no event.

F. Inside the transaction that ingests a delivery, and before any write, the receiver SHALL read its record of the delivery's channel and whether the channel is closed; SHALL refuse, with a channel-closed refusal naming the record, every delivery on a closed channel; SHALL acknowledge, appending no event, a delivery whose number and hash equal that record; SHALL refuse, with an out-of-sequence refusal naming the record, every other delivery whose number is not the record's number plus one or whose link is not the record's hash; SHALL refuse a delivery carrying an event whose provenance holds more than one entry or whose originator entry does not name the channel's sender database; and SHALL refuse, with a sender-superseded refusal naming the successor, a delivery from a sender database that a succession the receiver's log holds names as predecessor.

G. In the transaction that accepts a delivery, the receiver SHALL append exactly one reserved ingest audit of event type `ingest.delivery_accepted` whose data carries exactly `database_id` (the receiver), `channel`, `delivery_number`, `delivery_hash`, `previous_delivery_hash`, `event_ids`, `event_hashes` (the identifier and the hash of each event the delivery carried, as carried and in its order) and `parent_withheld` (as the delivery carried it), and SHALL record a `delivery` object carrying exactly the channel, `delivery_number` and `parent_withheld` (the delivery's boolean for that event) in the provenance entry it stamps on each event it ingests from that delivery.

H. The receiver's record of a channel SHALL be the `delivery_number` and `delivery_hash` of the `ingest.delivery_accepted` audit with the highest number among those naming that channel that the receiver authored, or number 0 and a null hash when there is none; a channel SHALL be closed when the receiver's log holds a skip event it accepted whose `recovered_channels` names the channel under a registration other than the skip event's own, or an `ingest.channel_closed` audit the receiver authored naming it; the receiver SHALL keep a working copy of each channel's record and closure, written only in the transaction that ingests a delivery of that channel, or that appends the closure, and read by assertion F's check; and the library SHALL offer an operation that derives every channel's record and closure from the log, reports each channel whose working copy differs from them, and reports each channel whose accepted audits, in number order, do not run from 1 with each audit's `previous_delivery_hash` equal to the `delivery_hash` of the audit before it.

I. The receiver endpoint SHALL answer an accepted delivery, and a re-presented one, with an acknowledgement carrying exactly `channel`, `receiver_database_id`, `record` (an object with exactly `delivery_number` and `delivery_hash`) and `outcome` (`accepted` or `represented`), and a refused delivery with a refusal carrying exactly `channel`, `receiver_database_id`, `record`, `refusal` (`out_of_sequence`, `channel_closed`, `sender_superseded`, `succession_refused` or `fork_unrecorded`), `successor_database_id` (the successor for `sender_superseded`, otherwise null) and `refused_event_id` (for `fork_unrecorded`, the event ingest refused; otherwise null); and the library SHALL provide the decoder that maps such a body to the send outcome it states.

J. For a destination that serializes natively, the drainer SHALL mark the head sent only on an accepting outcome whose record carries the delivery number and delivery hash it sent; SHALL handle an accepting outcome carrying another record as a receiver record returned for that delivery; SHALL handle a channel refusal other than `fork_unrecorded` as a receiver record returned for that delivery, never as a permanent failure; SHALL wedge the head with cause `fork_unrecorded`, recording the refused event, on a `fork_unrecorded` refusal; and SHALL wedge the head with cause `acknowledgement_invalid`, in the transaction that records the attempt, on an accepting outcome that carries no record.

K. After the transaction of a refused delivery rolls back, the receiver endpoint SHALL append, in a transaction of its own, a reserved ingest audit of event type `ingest.delivery_refused` whose data carries exactly `database_id` (the receiver), `channel`, `offered` (an object with exactly `delivery_number`, `previous_delivery_hash` and `delivery_hash`), `record` and `refusal`, unless the latest `ingest.delivery_refused` audit the receiver authored for that channel carries the same `offered`, `record` and `refusal`, in which case it SHALL append nothing.

L. The library's default delivery-channels view SHALL KEY its rows by the receiver's database identity, the sender's database identity, the destination identifier and the registration identifier that an `ingest.delivery_accepted`, `ingest.delivery_refused` or `ingest.channel_closed` audit names, and SHALL TREAT the latest accepted audit of a channel as its last delivery, the latest refusal audit as its last refusal, and a closure audit as closing it, deriving its rows solely from those audits.

M. The receiver endpoint's delivery, pull and report operations SHALL each take, from the deployment's authentication, the set of sender database identities the caller may act for, and SHALL refuse, before any read of a channel and any write, a delivery, a pull or a report naming a channel whose sender database is not in that set.

N. The receiver endpoint's pull SHALL return the receiver's database identity, the receiver's record of the named channel and whether it is closed; for each delivery number it names within that record, the delivery hash of the receiver's `ingest.delivery_accepted` audit for it; and for each delivery of the range it asks for, the delivery's number, link, hash and withheld-parent record and, in the order that audit lists them, the stored record of each event it names; it SHALL answer, naming the delivery, that it cannot serve a delivery within its record for which it holds no such audit or does not hold every event the audit names; and it SHALL answer a pull naming a superseded sender with a sender-superseded refusal naming the successor.

O. The receiver endpoint's pull SHALL, when asked for the channels of a sender database, optionally under one destination identifier, list each channel of that sender its log records, closed ones included, with the receiver's record of it and whether it is closed.

P. The library SHALL provide the decoder that maps a pull response to one of: served, a transient failure, a permanent failure, or a sender-superseded refusal; and a destination's pull operation SHALL report through that decoder's outcomes.

Q. The library SHALL declare `ingest.delivery_accepted`, `ingest.delivery_refused` and `ingest.channel_closed` as event types of its reserved ingest audit entry type, and SHALL append them only through the receiver endpoint and ingest.

R. The receiver endpoint's report operation SHALL accept a report that the sender stopped a channel because another live database with its identity delivers on it, and SHALL append, in a transaction of its own, a reserved ingest audit of event type `ingest.channel_closed` whose data carries exactly `database_id` (the receiver), `channel`, `cause` (`channel_shared`) and `reported_record` (the sender channel record the reporting sender holds, in the record shape), unless the channel is already closed, in which case it SHALL append nothing.

### Rationale

**Why natively serializing destinations only, and why refuse one without a pull and a report (assertion A)?** The channel's fields travel in the library's own batch envelope, and only a receiver running this library derives a record from its log; a destination whose transform produces another format has no such receiver. A natively serializing destination whose transport cannot pull could never check in, so its channel would be held forever after the first acquisition of the drain lock, and one that cannot report could not close a shared channel; refusing it at registration makes the gap a start-up error instead of a silent stall.

**Why this delivery hash (assertion B)?** The hash must be recomputable by the receiver from the envelope and, later, by the sender and by any verifier from the receiver's log, where the envelope's bytes and its incidental fields (`batch_id`, `sent_at`, the sender's hop and identifier) are not kept. The channel, the number, the link, the hashes of the events carried and the withheld-parent record are all recorded in the receiver's accepted-delivery audit, so the hash is a function of what the log holds. A hash of the wire bytes is not used for this reason. The event hashes are the hashes as sent: for an event the sender authored, the hash it was appended under. The withheld-parent record is covered because a receiver marks aggregates by it and refuses a delivery it contradicts; a record changed in transit would set or hide a mark.

**Why number at the fence, not at enqueue (assertions C and D)?** A number fixed when an item is enqueued would be consumed by every item a recovery retires without delivering it -- the wedged head and the pending items behind it -- and the refill's items would then leave a gap the receiver refuses, with no exception path to explain it. The drainer sends one item at a time, and only after the previous one was acknowledged, so the next delivery's number is always the last acknowledged number plus one. Assigning it in the pre-send fence, from the sender channel record read in that transaction, makes it a function of the record: every retry of an item before the record changes carries the same number and link and so the same hash, and an item retired by a recovery consumes nothing. The fence already guards the send against a halt committed meanwhile (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/N`); it guards it against a changed record in the same way. Each attempt records the number and hash it carried, the send fence record names the delivery of the last send started, and the sent transition records the pair the item was acknowledged under, so the sender retains every delivery it made -- the evidence the common-point search and the adoption of an unacknowledged delivery read. A delivery number is unique along the receiver's accepted chain, not across the sender's queue: after the receiver fell behind, the refill's deliveries reuse numbers that superseded items already carry, and the rewind event names the numbers it superseded. The sender channel record also keeps the receiver database identity the receiver's latest response named, so the sender knows, without configuration, which of its channels reach one receiver (`EVS-DEV-delivery-resume/T`).

**Why these envelope fields (assertion E)?** The envelope names its channel, so the receiver knows which record to check it against, and states its number, link and hash, and the withheld-parent record of each event it carries. A delivery with no event is refused because it carries nothing a receiver holds for the sender, so no pull could ever serve it back as a delivery of the sender's events. A batch in the earlier format carries no channel; it is refused by name, and the format step is part of the data-format major step every change here belongs to (`EVS-DEV-version-compatibility/C`).

**Why this receiver check (assertion F)?** The check runs inside the ingest transaction so that two deliveries of one channel racing on two server instances serialize: the second sees the first's record and is re-presented or refused. A closed channel refuses everything, so neither of two clones, nor a database still holding a registration its restored self recovered, can extend it. A re-presentation of the last accepted delivery is acknowledged without appending anything: it is the retry of a delivery whose acknowledgement was lost, and the record already names it. Every other out-of-sequence delivery is refused with the record, which is all the sender needs to decide what to do. A delivery event with more than its originator entry, or from another database, is refused because a channel carries only what its sender authored (`EVS-PRD-destinations/C`): the recovery of the sender's own events relies on every delivery holding only events the sender authored, and the receiver enforces it rather than trusting the sender's fill.

**Why an accepted-delivery audit, and a stamp on each ingested event (assertion G)?** The record must be derivable from the log for every delivery, including one whose events the receiver already holds -- the usual case after a resume -- which adds no provenance entry to any event. One audit per accepted delivery, appended in the transaction that accepts it, records the delivery whatever its events were, lists what it carried in order with the hashes as sent (for an event already held, the stored copy's hash is the receiver's own re-stamped one, so the hash as sent is recorded here), and is an ordinary reserved event that a declarative view can fold. The audit also records the withheld-parent record, so the pull, the recovery and a verifier recompute the delivery hash from the receiver's log. The stamp in the receiver's provenance entry of each event it ingests records, on the event itself and under the event's hash, which delivery on which channel brought it and whether one of its parents was withheld, which the default views read to mark an aggregate possibly incomplete (`EVS-DEV-branch-conflicts/E`). A stamp alone would not serve: a delivery of duplicates leaves no stamp, and the declarative projection shapes cannot address provenance fields.

**Why a working copy, and a check that walks the chain (assertion H)?** Only audits the receiver authored count: a receiver that ingests another receiver's audits holds that receiver's records, not its own. Deriving the record from the log on every delivery would scan every audit the receiver holds. The working copy is written only in the transaction that appends the audit it summarizes, so the two agree at every commit, and a restore of the database restores both together. It is part of the library's persisted state, under the storage precondition (`EVS-PRD-destinations/L`). Comparing the copy with the highest audit alone would not find a copy changed directly that let a delivery off the chain be accepted and then agreed with the audit that acceptance wrote; the check therefore also walks each channel's accepted audits and reports any break of number or link, so the gap-free chain is verifiable from the log itself.

**Why check the acknowledgement's record, and wedge on a missing one (assertions I and J)?** The acknowledgement is the only evidence the receiver accepted a delivery. A transport that returns a success status and drops the body would, if accepted, mark items delivered that the receiver may not hold; the library cannot tell, so it does not mark the item, and it wedges with a cause that names the transport's failure rather than retrying without end. A refusal of the channel's sequence is not a permanent failure of the item: it states where the channel stands, and the drainer acts on that (`EVS-DEV-delivery-resume/B`) instead of wedging. A `fork_unrecorded` refusal is different: ingest refused an event that forks the sender's origin chain where no skip event the receiver holds lists it (`EVS-DEV-chain-verification/L`), which no realignment of the channel changes, so the drainer wedges with that cause and names the refused event. Every response names the receiver's database identity, which the sender channel record keeps.

**Why a refusal audit in its own transaction, coalesced (assertion K)?** The refused delivery's transaction rolls back, so nothing of it remains; the refusal is recorded afterwards, like a rejected batch, so the receiver's log shows which channel offered what against which record. An audit that does not commit loses only the record of the refusal: the refusal itself was returned, and the sender records what it did about it in its own log. A refusal identical to the channel's latest adds no information, and an authenticated sender stuck in a loop would otherwise grow the receiver's log without bound, so only a refusal that differs is appended.

**Why this default view (assertion L)?** Operators need to see which senders are behind, regressed, refused or closed. The view is a declarative Table view over the three receiver audits, so it is registered, folded and rebuilt like every other view. It is a Layer 2 convention, the library's reading of each channel's state; its `default` name says so. Its key includes the receiver's identity, so rows for audits a peer forwarded stay apart from the receiver's own, as the destination-wedges view keeps peers' rows apart. A closure by a skip event's recovered channels is read from the skip event by the receiver's check and its working copy; the view shows closures the receiver's own audits record.

**Why authenticate by sender identity (assertion M)?** The endpoint cannot know which channels a credential covers; the deployment's authentication does, and it states it as the set of sender database identities the caller may act for. A delivery, a pull or a report for any other sender is refused before anything about the channel is read, so a caller cannot learn another sender's record or close its channel. This widens the consumer-supplied wire-authentication trust input: it is trusted to bind a caller to the right sender identities, as it is trusted to map a credential to a principal.

**Why serve from the log, duplicates and deletions included (assertions N and O)?** A range served from current view rows would omit every entry deleted since and every event the view does not fold; the recovery would then restore a history that is not the one the sender sent. The log holds every event of every accepted delivery, the events recording deletions included, and the accepted-delivery audit the receiver appended when it accepted each delivery lists them in order. An event a delivery brought again after the receiver already held it is served from its stored copy, in the position the audit lists it, with the hash as sent that the audit records. A receiver that cannot reconstruct a delivery its record includes says so rather than serving a partial one, since a partial delivery would not recompute to its hash. Closed channels are still served, because their deliveries may hold the only copy of a sender's events. A superseded sender is refused on the pull as on delivery, so a still-live predecessor learns of its successor at its next check-in. The listing of a sender's channels lets a check-in find a lost channel of an earlier registration, and lets a successor, which has none of its predecessor's registration identifiers, find what to restore.

**Why typed pull outcomes, and declared audit types (assertions P and Q)?** The drainer holds a channel while its check-in fails transiently, and must stop, with a cause, when the pull fails in a way no retry changes -- a transport that reaches no receiver endpoint of this library -- rather than hold the channel for ever. The three receiver audits are event types of the reserved ingest audit entry type, so the event store's public append operations refuse them and ingest checks their shape (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/L`).

**Why a report operation (assertion R)?** Only a sender can detect that its channel is shared, from the skip events it recovers, and only the receiver can stop both databases, because it is the one place both deliver. The report carries the stop across; the receiver records it as its own audit, so the closure is derivable from its log and survives its restores exactly as far as the deliveries it closes. A second report of an already closed channel changes nothing and appends nothing.

## EVS-DEV-delivery-resume: Channel check-in, recovery and resume

**Level**: DEV | **Status**: Active | **Implements**: -
**Refines**: EVS-PRD-delivery-channel

### Purpose

This requirement holds what the drainer does with a receiver record: the check-in that holds a channel after each acquisition of the drain lock, the classification of a record against the sender's own and of the channels the receiver lists, the adoption of a delivery the receiver accepted whose outcome the sender did not record, the search for each recovered channel's common point, the recovery of the sender's own events and how they are stored, the resume through a halt of purpose `resume` and the resume recovery with the rewind or skip event it appends, how the branch point, abandoned head, forks and recovered and unrecovered positions are determined, the library's own stop of a channel through a halt of purpose `channel_stop`, the cancellation of a resume request the channel no longer needs, the report that closes a shared channel, and the hold of a channel while another channel to the same receiver is mid-resume.

### Assertions

A. The drainer SHALL start no send on a channel whose sender channel record does not hold, as its check-in epoch, the drain epoch of the lock the drainer holds. After each acquisition of the drain lock, and after the closing of a halt request or the recovery of a wedge that held back a check-in's decision on a channel (assertion I), it SHALL check in that channel by a pull naming the sender channel record's number and asking for the channels the receiver holds for the sending database under the destination's identifier; it SHALL keep the channel held, continuing to fill it and to honour halt requests on it, and SHALL repeat a check-in that fails transiently under the destination's retry policy, until a check-in response is received; it SHALL write the check-in epoch only in the transaction that commits the in-step decision, the adoption or the resume recovery the response calls for; and the library's read of delivery status SHALL report each held channel.

B. On a receiver record obtained from a check-in, an acknowledgement or a refusal, the drainer SHALL decide, in this order: a resume after recovery from the receiver, when the check-in lists a lost channel (a channel of the sending database under the destination's identifier and a registration other than the one the sender holds, that the receiver does not report closed and that no resume event the sender holds names in its `recovered_channels`); in step, when the record equals the sender channel record; adopted, when the record's number exceeds the sender channel record's and the record's number and hash are those of an attempt the sender retains or of the delivery its send fence record names; receiver behind, when the record's number is below the sender channel record's and the record is number 0 or the sender retains a delivery with the record's number and hash; and otherwise a resume after recovery from the receiver.

C. On an adopted record the drainer SHALL, in one transaction, set the sender channel record to it and, when the adopted delivery is the pending head's latest attempt or the delivery its send fence record names for that head, mark the head sent under that delivery.

D. Before recovering, the drainer SHALL determine, for the current channel and for each lost channel, the common point as the highest delivery number at which the receiver's log and a delivery the sender retains under that channel's registration (a sent item, a recorded attempt, or the delivery its send fence record names) hold the same delivery hash, from the hashes the receiver's pull reports for the numbers the drainer names, or delivery 0 when there is none.

E. The drainer SHALL recover, for each channel it recovers (the current channel when its record calls for a recovery, and each lost channel), in delivery-number order, every delivery the receiver's pull serves numbered above that channel's common point and up to the receiver's record of it, and SHALL store an event of a delivery only when the deliveries up to that one form an unbroken chain by link from the common point's hash (the null link for delivery 0), the delivery recomputes to its hash, and every event it carries passes ingest's integrity verification, names the sender's own database identity in its originator entry, carries exactly one entry after it, the receiver's, whose arrival hash is the hash the delivery lists for that event, and, with that entry removed and its origin sequence number restored, hashes to that arrival hash; and it SHALL resume only once, on every channel it recovers, the delivery whose hash is the receiver's record of that channel has been verified so.

F. The recovery SHALL store, in ascending order of origin position across every channel it recovers, each event of a verified delivery that the sender does not hold, as the receiver served it with the sender's own provenance entry appended (naming the sender's database identity and recording the channel and delivery number it was recovered from), under a new local sequence number, in transactions that each store a whole number of served events; SHALL store nothing for an event the sender holds with the hash the receiver's entry records as its arrival hash, whichever run or channel stored it; and SHALL enqueue no recovered event on any destination.

G. The drainer SHALL stop a channel, storing nothing further from the pull, with the cause `recovery_refused` and the kind of failure when a served delivery fails a verification of assertion E, when the receiver answers that it cannot serve a delivery within its record, or when the sender holds an event the pull serves under another hash; with the cause `channel_shared` when a served delivery carries a skip event, appended by a database with the sender's identity, that the sender does not hold and whose `abandoned_head` is an event the sender authored after the latest skip event it authored for that channel, or when the pull reports the channel closed; with the cause `check_in_refused` when the check-in's pull reports a permanent failure; and with the cause `sender_superseded` when the pull reports that the receiver superseded the sender.

H. The drainer SHALL stop a channel by appending a halt request of purpose `channel_stop` recording the cause, the kind of failure where assertion G names one, and the receiver record, and SHALL honour that request at the channel's pending head by wedging it with the recorded cause; when the database holds an uncovered recovered event (an event it recovered as its own whose origin position no skip event it authored records), the transaction that appends that request SHALL also append a skip event carrying the keys of assertion L over everything the database has recovered, with `resume_after`, `previous_record`, `common_point`, `superseded_deliveries` and `fill_position` null, which the library SHALL enqueue on none of the stopped channel's queue and on the database's other channels as it enqueues every skip event; while that request is open or its wedge stands, the drainer SHALL keep the channel held and SHALL not check it in; and after an operator's cancellation of that request or recovery of that wedge it SHALL check the channel in again.

I. When a receiver record calls for a resume and the channel's queue head is pending, the drainer SHALL append a halt request of purpose `resume`, recording the receiver record, in the transaction that commits the outcome or the check-in that returned it, and SHALL honour it by wedging the head with cause `channel_resume`; while another halt request is open for the destination or its head is wedged, it SHALL request no resume, leaving the check-in's decision held back until that request is closed or that wedge recovered; and, when the channel has no pending head, SHALL run the resume recovery with no halt.

J. The resume recovery SHALL run in one transaction of the drainer that verifies the drain lock; SHALL refuse unless the head is wedged with cause `channel_resume` or the queue has no pending head; and SHALL tombstone that wedged head, delete the pending items behind it, rewind the fill position below the lowest event carried by every item it removes and by every retained item whose recorded delivery number is above the current channel's common point and whose delivery the receiver's log does not hold, set the sender channel record to the receiver record obtained under the current drain epoch and record that record as the resume record, remove the wedge record, append the recovery event, append a skip event when the pull served any delivery above the common point of a channel the resume recovered and a rewind event otherwise, and enqueue that resume event, alone, as the destination's next queue item, whatever the destination's filter.

K. The rewind event SHALL carry exactly `id` (the destination), `database_id` (the sender), `registration_id`, `halt_request_event_id` (the resume request the wedge consumed, null when none), `resume_after` (the receiver record, as an object with exactly `delivery_number` and `delivery_hash`), `previous_record` (the sender channel record before the resume, in that shape), `common_point` (the current channel's, in that shape), `superseded_deliveries` (the ascending delivery numbers above the common point whose retained items it rewound below), `fill_position` (the position it rewound to) and `drainer_epoch`.

L. The skip event SHALL carry exactly the keys of the rewind event and `recovered_channels` (for each channel whose deliveries the resume recovered, in ascending order of registration identifier, an object with exactly `registration_id`, `common_point` and `recovered_through`, the receiver's record of that channel, each point in the record shape), `recovered_sequences` and `recovered_count` (the ascending inclusive ranges `[first, last]` of the origin sequence numbers of every event, carried by the served deliveries above the common point of a recovered channel, that the sender holds through any recovery of its own events, whichever run or channel stored it, and their number), `unrecovered_sequences` (the ascending inclusive ranges of origin sequence positions above the branch point and up to the abandoned head at which the sender holds no event of the abandoned branch), `branch_point` and `abandoned_head` (each an object with exactly `sequence_number`, the event's origin position, and `event_hash`, its sealed hash, both null when the sender holds as authored every event the served deliveries carry), `forks` (for each predecessor value, null included, that more than one event of the sender's origin chain the sender holds carries, other than the skip event itself, in ascending order of the predecessor's origin position with null first, an object with exactly `predecessor`, null or an object with exactly `sequence_number` and `event_hash`, and `successors`, every such event carrying it, in ascending order of origin position and then sealed hash, each as an object with exactly `sequence_number` and `event_hash`), `conflicted_aggregates` and `possibly_incomplete_aggregates`.

M. The drainer SHALL determine the abandoned head as the event with the highest origin sequence number among the events of the abandoned branch the sender holds; the branch point as the event the sender holds as authored at the highest origin sequence position below the abandoned head at which the abandoned branch is proven to hold that same event, by a served event with that position and that event's identity and hash, or by the predecessor link of the lowest-positioned event of the abandoned branch; and an event of the abandoned branch as a served event of any recovered channel that the sender does not hold as authored, or an event the sender holds through any recovery whose originator entry names its own identity.

N. The library SHALL declare the rewind event, the skip event and the succession event as reserved destination audit entry types, `system.destination_delivery_rewound`, `system.destination_delivery_skipped` and `system.destination_sender_succeeded`, each with an event type of its own.

O. A receiver SHALL accept a rewind or skip event that names the channel of the delivery carrying it only when its `resume_after` is not null, and only as the only event of the delivery numbered one after the record it names in `resume_after`, whose link is that record's hash; and in the transaction that accepts a skip event, it SHALL record closed every channel the event's `recovered_channels` names under a registration other than the event's own.

P. A receiver record returned for a delivery sent after a resume, equal to that resume's record, SHALL wedge the head with cause `repeated_gap_refusal`; a channel-closed refusal of a delivery SHALL wedge the head with cause `channel_shared`; a sender-superseded refusal of a delivery SHALL wedge the head with cause `sender_superseded`; and a succession refusal SHALL wedge it with cause `succession_refused`; and the drainer SHALL request no resume for any of them, nor for a `fork_unrecorded` refusal.

Q. Each channel's check-in, hold, recovery, stop and resume SHALL proceed independently of every other channel's, except as assertion T holds a channel for another channel to the same receiver, and a recovery SHALL skip an event another channel's recovery already stored.

R. When a check-in or an acknowledgement obtained under a drain epoch later than that of an open halt request of purpose `resume` finds the channel in step or adopts a record, the drainer SHALL, in the transaction that commits that decision, close the request with a cancellation event it appends itself, and SHALL not wedge the head for it.

S. After it stops a channel with cause `channel_shared` on a skip event it recovered, the drainer SHALL report the stop through the destination's report operation, repeating a report that fails transiently under the destination's retry policy until it is received.

T. The drainer SHALL start no send on a channel while another channel of the sending database whose sender channel record names the same receiver database identity has not committed its check-in decision under the current drain epoch, has an open halt request of purpose `resume` or `channel_stop` or a standing wedge from one, or has a resume event it has not had acknowledged; and the library's read of delivery status SHALL report each channel so held and the channel it waits for.

### Rationale

**Why hold per channel, keyed by the drain epoch (assertion A)?** The drain epoch rises at every acquisition of the drain lock (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain-lock/A`), so a check-in epoch equal to the holder's epoch is exactly "checked in since this drainer took the lock", readable in the fence transaction without a clock or a process-local flag that a crash would lose. The hold is per channel so that a receiver that does not answer holds only its own channel (`EVS-PRD-destinations/G`). The fill and halt honour go on during a hold: neither sends anything, and a queue that grows while held is delivered once the check-in answers. The epoch is written only by the transaction that realigns the channel, so a drainer that stops part way through a recovery checks in again at its next acquisition and resumes from what the receiver says then. A check-in whose decision an operator's halt or a standing wedge held back is repeated once that is cleared: the operator's recovery refills the channel, but the channel is still held, and without a new check-in it would stay held until the next acquisition, which on a long-running server may never come. A held channel is reported in the delivery status (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/T`), so an operator sees why nothing is delivering.

**Why this order of decisions (assertion B)?** Each case is decided by evidence the sender holds. A lost channel comes first: whatever the current channel's record says, the receiver holds deliveries of the sender that the sender lacks, and sending on the current channel before recovering them would put post-restore events on the wire with no record of the fork. A record equal to the sender's needs nothing. A record ahead of the sender's that names a delivery the sender made is the receiver's acknowledgement of that delivery, lost in transit, lost with the drain lock before its outcome committed (`EVS-PRD-destinations/J`), or retired by a recovery before its outcome was known: the channel adopts it, which keeps the retry of such a delivery from reading as a regression. The send fence record names the delivery of the last send started, so even an attempt whose outcome never committed is recognised. A record the sender's retained deliveries contain, below its own, is a receiver that fell behind. Anything else means the receiver holds deliveries the sender does not: the sender went back in time, or both did, or a second live source delivered.

**Why adopt, and mark the head sent (assertion C)?** A delivery the receiver accepted and the sender made is delivered, whatever the sender's queue recorded. When it is the pending head's, marking the head sent is the outcome the lost acknowledgement would have committed. When it belongs to an item a recovery already retired, the retired item's events were enqueued again by the refill, and the receiver deduplicates them.

**Why a common point for each channel (assertion D)?** When both ends went back in time to different points, the receiver's deliveries above some number differ from the sender's retained ones with the same numbers. The common point is where the two chains last agree; the receiver's deliveries above it are what the sender recovers, and the sender's deliveries above it that the receiver's log does not hold are what the refill delivers again. The chains share a prefix, so the drainer can narrow the search by asking the receiver for its hashes at numbers it names; delivery 0 always agrees. A lost channel's common point is usually 0, since the restored sender holds no registration of it; it is higher only when a sender that deleted and registered the destination again kept the terminal items of the earlier registration. The search reads retained deliveries by number and hash together, because after a receiver fell behind, the sender's queue holds superseded items that share numbers with later deliveries.

**Why these verifications before storing (assertion E)?** The recovery admits events that name the sender's own identity, which every ingest entry point refuses (`EVS-PRD-ingest/H`), so it admits only what is consistent with its own channel. The chain from the common point to the record binds the served deliveries to the sender's own delivery history; each recomputed hash binds a delivery to its events; ingest's integrity verification binds each event to the hops it passed; and the reconstruction of the record as sent, compared with the hash the delivery lists for it, shows the event is the one the receiver accepted on this channel. These checks do not show that the sender authored the event: the hashes are unkeyed, so a receiver can build a consistent history under the sender's identity, and the receiver is trusted not to (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel`, Trust). The resume waits for the delivery each record names, so it never realigns the channel past a delivery the receiver claims and has not shown.

**Why store recovered events this way (assertion F)?** A recovered event keeps its identity, its hash (as its first receiver entry's arrival hash) and its predecessor link, and gains the sender's own entry, so the log reads that the sender recovered it, from which channel and delivery. Its origin sequence position may be one the sender's post-restore events already reuse, and the local sequence number is the storage key, so it is stored under a new local sequence number, as ingest stores every event, in origin order across the channels it recovers, so the abandoned branch keeps its write order in the log; the origin position stays in the receiver's entry. The views leave it out until the resume's skip event fixes which aggregates are in conflict (`EVS-DEV-branch-conflicts/F`): folding it as an ordinary ingested event would put the abandoned branch's state over the edits made after the restore. It is not an event the sender authored in this history, so no destination enqueues it (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/V`), the channel it came from included. Short transactions, each storing whole events, keep a long recovery from holding one transaction open, and a recovery interrupted part way is repeated at the next check-in: events already stored are held under the same hash and are skipped.

**Why stop, and why these causes (assertion G)?** Each is a condition no resume changes. A served delivery that does not verify, or one the receiver's record includes and the receiver cannot serve, means the receiver is not serving what the channel carried; resuming past it would skip deliveries on the receiver's word alone. A skip event of the sender's identity that the sender did not append, abandoning what the sender authored after its own latest skip on the channel, means another database took the channel back from this one after this one had taken it (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel/N`); resuming would take it back again, and the two would alternate for ever. A database that was restored and holds no skip after the abandoned events meets a skip of its own later history, not this pattern, and resumes. A channel the receiver reports closed was closed over a shared identity or recovered by a restored self. A pull that fails permanently reaches no receiver endpoint of this library, and a superseded sender has a successor.

**Why stop through a halt request (assertion H)?** Only the drainer wedges a head, and only a pending one (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/N`), but a check-in on an idle channel has no head to wedge. The stop is therefore a halt request, an event that records the cause at once, whether or not a head exists; the drainer honours it at the first head the fill produces, as it honours any request, and the wedge carries the cause the request recorded. The channel stays held meanwhile, so nothing is sent, and the drainer stops asking the receiver until an operator has cancelled the request or recovered the wedge, after which it asks again: an operator who fixed the receiver, or retired the second live database, needs no other step, and one whose queue is empty need not wait for a head. A stop can come after the recovery stored part of the abandoned branch. The skip appended with the stop records what was recovered, the forks it opened and the conflicts it made, so the database's views and chain reach a recorded state without waiting for an operator; the skip the eventual resume appends records at least as much.

**Why a halt of purpose `resume`, and none on an empty queue (assertion I)?** The halt and its wedge are the library's way of stopping a drainer at a head without racing a send (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/N`), and they record the stop in the log. The request records the receiver record that called for the resume, so the log reads why the library halted delivery. A resume is not requested over an operator's open request or a wedge an operator must resolve; the decision waits, and the check-in is repeated once that request or wedge is cleared (assertion A). A channel with no pending head has no send to stop, so the resume recovery runs at once; waiting for the next local event to give it a head would leave a receiver that fell behind without the deliveries it lost.

**Why this recovery transaction (assertion J)?** It is the operator's recovery (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/F`) with the channel realigned in the same transaction: the items queued before the regression was detected are retired and refilled, numbered after the receiver record, and the deliveries the receiver's log does not hold are rewound below so that the refill delivers their events again under the configuration in effect. The receiver record it realigns to is the one obtained under the current drain epoch, so a drainer that takes over a resume another began realigns to what the receiver says now. It appends the ordinary recovery event, so the default destination-wedges view ends the wedge as for any recovery, and one resume event, which it enqueues alone ahead of the refill whatever the filter, so that the receiver receives it first. One resume event covers every channel the resume recovered, the lost ones included, so the resumed channel carries a single explanation at a single place. The fill never enqueues the resume event on the channel it names (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/V`), so the receiver never meets it twice.

**Why these rewind and skip fields (assertions K and L)?** They state, from the sender's log alone, what the resume did: which channel, after which receiver record, from which sender record, which deliveries it superseded and how far it rewound. The skip event adds what the conflicts and chain verification build on: which channels it recovered and how far, which origin positions came back and how many, which abandoned positions nobody holds, where the origin chain branched and where the abandoned branch ended, every fork of the sender's origin chain with its successors by name, the aggregates both branches wrote a version of, and the aggregates whose recovered state may be incomplete because a channel's filter could have left out events that fold into them. The branch point, abandoned head and fork points are recorded as origin position and sealed hash, because the sender's stored hash of a recovered event is its own re-stamp, which no receiver holds. The recovered positions are those the sender holds for the served deliveries, whichever run or channel stored them, so a recovery interrupted and repeated, or one that another channel's recovery ran ahead of, records the same content. They are recorded as ranges of origin positions, not one by one, because a recovery can bring back thousands, and each recovered event already names, in the sender's own entry, the channel and delivery it came from. The conflict records, the forks and the possibly-incomplete list are computed over every event of the abandoned branch the sender holds, from every recovery so far. The conflict records leave out events a reconciliation already superseded, so a later skip event on another channel reopens only what the user has not yet reconciled (`EVS-DEV-branch-conflicts`).

**Why this branch point (assertion M)?** The branch point is the last event both branches share. The sender can prove an event shared only where the abandoned branch shows it: a served event it already holds as authored at that position, or the predecessor link of the lowest abandoned event, which names the event before it. Between the last proven shared position and the first abandoned event, a position the channel's filter left out can be shared or abandoned, and nothing the channel carried says which. The branch point is therefore the highest proven shared event, which may be lower than the true fork and never higher: an event counted after the branch point that both branches in fact share can only add an aggregate to the conflict set, never hide a conflict. The positions from there to the abandoned head that no one holds are listed, so a verifier knows which links of the abandoned branch it cannot follow. A database's sequence counter numbers every event it stores, the events it ingested included, so a listed unrecovered position can be one at which the sender stored an ingested event; the receiver never had such an event, and a verifier treats the position as one it cannot follow. A predecessor link names only an event the sender authored (`EVS-DEV-chain-verification/B`), so the predecessor link of the lowest abandoned event names an event of the sender's own origin chain. When the sender holds that event, the branch point is exact. When it does not, the authored events between the branch point and the lowest abandoned event may be shared or continuing, and the conflict records list them as ambiguous (`EVS-DEV-branch-conflicts/A`).

**Why reserved destination audits (assertion N)?** Each records an operation of the library on one destination of one database, so each carries the destination and the database identity, and the shape rules and ingest checks for destination audits apply to it (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/H`, `K`, `L`); a receiver that holds one from another channel checks it like any other destination audit.

**Why a receiver checks the resume event's place, and closes recovered lost channels (assertion O)?** The resume event states the record the channel continues after; accepting it anywhere else on its own channel would record a resume the channel did not make. A skip appended with a stop names no record to continue after, and is never enqueued on the channel it names, so the check applies only to a skip that resumes. On a sender's other channels a skip event is an audit delivered in queue order, and it names another channel, so the check does not apply to it. A lost channel the skip recovered belongs to a registration the sender no longer holds, and the receiver closes it in the same transaction, so nothing extends it afterwards (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel/W`).

**Why wedge on the record already resumed from, on a closed channel, and on supersession (assertion P)?** A resume realigns the channel to the receiver's record; the same record returned for the next delivery shows that the realignment did not take, and a second resume would loop. A channel the receiver closed, a receiver that refuses a superseded sender, one that refuses a succession, or one that refuses an unlisted fork states a fact about the sender that no resume can change. Each wedge records its cause, and waits for an operator.

**Why independent channels (assertion Q)?** A sender with two channels to two receivers may learn of its restore on one long before the other answers. Holding one channel's resume for another's would let an unreachable receiver stop delivery to a reachable one. Each channel's skip event records what the sender held of the abandoned branch by then; a later recovery on another channel may bring back more, which its own skip event records together with everything before it, and a recovery never stores an event twice. Until the later skip reaches it, a receiver can only know the conflicts the skips it holds record. Channels to one receiver are the exception (assertion T).

**Why cancel a resume request the channel no longer needs (assertion R)?** A drainer can append a resume request and lose the drain lock before honouring it; the next holder checks in and may find the channel in step, or adopt a record. Honouring the stale request would wedge a head, retire and refill the queue, and record a resume the channel did not make. The drainer that finds the channel realigned under a later epoch closes the request with a cancellation it appends itself, so the log reads that the library withdrew its own request and why nothing was resumed.

**Why report a shared channel (assertion S)?** The drainer that detects the sharing is the only one that knows of it; its report is what lets the receiver close the channel for the other database too (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel/X`). It is repeated until received, because until the receiver holds it, the other database keeps delivering.

**Why hold a channel for another channel to the same receiver (assertion T)?** A receiver's ingest accepts a second successor of a sender's predecessor only once it holds the skip that lists it (`EVS-DEV-chain-verification/L`). On one channel the skip is the first delivery after the resume. But a receiver can serve two of a sender's channels: one resumed after recovering abandoned events, and the other in step, holding continuing events queued ahead of any skip. If the second sent first, the receiver would hold the abandoned event from the first channel, see the continuing event as an unrecorded fork, and refuse it. Holding the second channel until the first has decided, and has had its resume event acknowledged, delivers the explanation first. A stopped channel holds the others to its receiver too, so that an operator's resolution precedes any fork there. The receiver identity comes from the receiver's own responses, so the sender needs no configuration to know which channels share a receiver.

## EVS-DEV-sender-succession: Sender succession

**Level**: DEV | **Status**: Active | **Implements**: -
**Refines**: EVS-PRD-delivery-channel

### Purpose

This requirement holds how a fresh database restores a predecessor sender's deliveries from a receiver, when and with what data the library appends the succession event and how it is delivered, how a receiver accepts or refuses it, what accepting it changes at the receiver, and the lineage the library derives from succession events.

### Assertions

A. The library SHALL offer a restore operation that, through the pull of a destination registered in the successor, lists the channels the receiver holds for a named predecessor database, closed ones included, pulls each listed channel's deliveries from 1 up to the receiver's record of it, and stores each carried event the successor does not hold, in ascending order of origin position across every channel it restores, under ordinary ingest's refusals, with the successor's provenance entry appended recording the channel and delivery number it was pulled from; and SHALL store nothing of a delivery unless the channel's deliveries chain by link from delivery 1 (whose link is null), each recomputes to its hash, and every event it carries passes ingest's integrity verification, names the predecessor in its originator entry, and carries exactly one entry after it, the receiver's, whose arrival hash is the hash the delivery lists for that event.

B. The library SHALL append the succession event only in the transaction in which the restore operation stores the last delivery of the last channel it restores, with data carrying exactly `id` and `registration_id` (the successor's destination the restore pulled through), `database_id` (the successor), `predecessor_database_id`, and `predecessor_channels` (for each channel restored, an object with exactly `destination_id`, `registration_id`, `delivery_number` and `delivery_hash`, the last delivery restored); SHALL record, in that transaction, a succession delivery request naming that registration and the succession event; SHALL refuse a restore naming the successor's own identity, a predecessor that a succession the successor's log holds already names, or a predecessor for which the receiver lists no channel; and SHALL append no succession event when an event the successor authored touches an aggregate that an event it restored touches.

C. A receiver SHALL accept a succession event only when every channel it names is one its log records for the predecessor with a record equal to the named delivery, the channels it names are every channel its log records for the predecessor, closed ones included, no succession its log holds already names the predecessor, and the endpoint's caller may act for both the successor and the predecessor; otherwise it SHALL refuse the delivery with a succession refusal.

D. The library SHALL offer a read of the succession lineage of a sender database identity, derived solely from the succession events the log holds: the predecessors it succeeded, transitively, and its successor, if any.

### Rationale

**Why restore every channel, verified from delivery 1 (assertion A)?** The successor holds none of its predecessor's history, so it cannot anchor the chain at a delivery of its own as a resuming sender does; delivery 1's null link is the anchor, and the chain from it to the receiver's record binds what is served to the predecessor's whole delivery history on that channel as the receiver holds it. The anchor binds consistency, not authorship: the receiver is trusted not to fabricate the predecessor's history (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel`, Trust). The receiver lists every channel it holds for the predecessor, earlier registrations and closed channels included, because a successor has none of the registration identifiers and a closed channel can hold the only copy of some of the predecessor's events. The events are the predecessor's, not the successor's own, so ordinary ingest's refusals apply and the own-event refusal does not arise; they keep their identity and hash, and the successor's entry records that they were pulled, from where. They are stored in origin order across channels, so the predecessor's write order per aggregate holds in the successor's log (`EVS-PRD-event-log/C`). A predecessor's skip events are stored at their origin positions, which follow most of the events they list, so the restore applies the fork refusal of ordinary ingest (`EVS-DEV-chain-verification/L`) once the whole restore has been stored, in the transaction that appends the succession event, rather than event by event. They are not authored by the successor, so no destination of the successor enqueues them.

**Why only in the completing transaction, delivered through a request (assertion B)?** The succession event claims that the successor holds the predecessor's deliveries up to the ones it names, obtained from the receiver. Appending it only in the transaction that stores the last of them makes the claim true at its commit, and leaves no operation through which a caller can claim it otherwise: the event store's public append operations refuse every reserved entry type (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/L`). A succession for its own identity, a second one for the same predecessor, or one with nothing restored would name no predecessor data the successor obtained. A successor that already authored events on an aggregate its predecessor's restored events touch has two authors on that aggregate with no branch point between them; declaring continuity would hide that, so the restore keeps the events it stored and appends no succession, and the successor's events on that aggregate remain a second editor's. Only the drainer enqueues (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/E`), and the restore is not the drainer, so the restore records a request in the same transaction and the drainer's next fill enqueues the succession event on the channel it restored through, whatever the filter, because that receiver must learn of it to close the predecessor's channels; its other channels receive it as the fill delivers skip events (`EVS-DEV-destination-drain/V`).

**Why these receiver checks (assertion C)?** A succession is accepted only when it describes the receiver's own records exactly: each named channel's record equals the named delivery, so the predecessor delivered nothing after the successor restored (a predecessor that did is still live); and every channel of the predecessor is named, so no channel is left open. A predecessor already succeeded has a successor; a later successor names that successor as its predecessor. The caller must be authenticated for both identities, so one sender's credential cannot take over another's data. After acceptance the receiver refuses the predecessor's later deliveries (`EVS-DEV-delivery-channel/F`).

**Why a lineage read (assertion D)?** The Layer 2 interpretation that a successor continues its predecessor's authorship (`EVS-PRD-delivery-channel/U`) needs one reading of which identities form a lineage, shared by the verification of origin chains and causal parents, by the branch-conflict rules and by any canonicalization rule; deriving it solely from succession events keeps it closed under events.
